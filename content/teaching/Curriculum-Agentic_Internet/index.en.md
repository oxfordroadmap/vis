## ⚡TLDR

The `{{% param "emoji" %}}{{% param "curriculum" %}}` curriculum guides learners through the frontier of AI-integrated web systems, emphasizing agentic design, multilingual adaptability, and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) logic. Through hands-on modules and impact-driven projects, students learn not only how to build web applications—but how to design intelligence, model decision contexts, and anticipate futures within ethical, participatory, and data-responsive frameworks.

📚 The curriculum unfolds in **5 progressive stages** across **16+2 modules**, adaptable to semester-based or self-paced formats:

- 🧭 **Stage 1: Conceptual Foundation** — _🌐 TECHC, 📡 INTRO, ✍️ COPYE_: Students build critical digital literacy through open-source exploration, version-controlled writing, and sociotechnical reflection—laying the groundwork for structured communication and networked authorship using 🐙 GitHub.
    
- 🦾 **Stage 2: Practical Foundation** — _🐙 AG101, ♞🧱 AG201, 📊 INFOV, 🧮 ONSUR_: Students apply modular web design, prompt engineering, visual storytelling, and data analysis to build agentic systems. Portfolios evolve through agile workflows, multilingual interfaces, and contextual surveys—all hosted in or linked from 🐙 GitHub.
    
- 🔁 **Stage 3: Front-end Production** — _🤗 AG102, ⚙️ FRONT, 🐍 PYTHN, 🔍 USRES_:  Stage 3 empowers learners to build reactive applications and interface logic using modular front-end design, lightweight Python scripting, and UX-driven research. Students debug frameworks, scaffold agentic features, and interpret data with foundational dashboards—bridging user context with LLM-enhanced outputs via platforms such as 🤗 Hugging Face.
    
- ⛓️ **Stage 4: Data-heavy Production** — _♝🍞 AG202, ♛🖼️ AG203, 🎨 UIVIS, 💡 PLNFM, 🧠 UXANA_: Learners advance into data-intensive agentic systems, integrating Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines, visual dashboards, and platform-aware design. They construct multilingual interfaces, interpret user behavior, and align agentic outputs with stakeholder needs—bridging structured knowledge with adaptive media through platforms such as 🤗 Hugging Face and 🐙 GitHub.
    
- 🚀 **Stage 5: Capstone Impact** — _AG302 & AG303_: Students synthesize their work into public-facing portfolios, conduct science mapping, and articulate AI impact using forecasting and responsible innovation tools.
    

### ✨ Highlights

- 🔧 **Build** agentic web apps integrating LLMs via 🤗 Hugging Face and 🐙 GitHub for real-world functionality
    
- 📦 **Deliver and deploy** structured expressive intelligence through multilingual content, structured data, PRDs, and agentic workflows
    
- 🔗 **Synthesize and forecast** data, decisions, and storytelling into ethical, adaptive dashboards for public-facing engagement—including code sharing and science mapping
    

### 🎯 Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- 🖌️ **Construct** structured web applications and interactive LLM-powered systems using modern web frameworks
    
- 📑 **Compose** AI agent product requirements documents (PRDs) structured for code-generation and aligned with professional standards, including W3C accessibility and sustainable web design principles
    
- 🧰 **Communicate** project decisions and value propositions across relevant ecosystems and Communities of Practice (CoPs)
    
- 🎨 **Design** data visualizations and agentic interfaces that respond to user context and ethical considerations
    
- 🧪 **Evaluate** innovation scenarios and impact through AI assessment methods, science mapping, responsible AI frameworks, and foresight methodologies

## 🔖 Curriculum Details

Progressively enriching learners' portfolios through project documentation and interpretive outcomes, this curriculum guides students through a three-stage learning journey. Each stage deepens their technical fluency, innovative imagination, agentic design capacity, and ethical considerations—***regardless of prior programming experience***. Learners begin by mastering Web fundamentals, then evolve agentic interfaces, and finally synthesize innovations with societal impact.

This five-stage curriculum begins with three foundational courses and builds toward two final capstone projects, moving from basic skills to advanced, real-world applications.

### 🧭 Stage 1: Foundation — _🌐 TECHC, 📡 INTRO, & ✍️ COPYE_

Stage 1 introduces learners to the foundational literacies of the agentic internet era. Through collaborative inquiry and scaffolded media creation, students explore sociotechnical systems, practice version-controlled writing, and build personal documentation ecosystems.

#### 🌐 TECHC: Internet & Intelligent Agent Technologies and Collaboration
- 🧠 **Essential Ecosystem Skills**: Students explore internet infrastructures, open-source platforms, and collaborative frameworks through sociotechnical case studies of emerging popular agentic development projects.
- 🐙 **GitHub Versioning**: Students use GitHub for version control, collaborative editing, and basic file structuring across repositories and markdown-based notebooks.
- 📦 **Delivery & Demonstration**: Students deliver a personal portfolio website, using a simple landing page with a Call-to-Action (CTA) to introduce themselves and their learning objectives.

####  📡 INTRO: Introduction to AI Agents
- 🧠 **Core Concepts of Digital Communication**: Students engage with intelligent agent frameworks, exploring how AI systems interpret, generate, and mediate information within digital platforms.    
- 🔍 **Histories & Futures of Agency**: Learners examine sociotechnical models of autonomy, decision-making, and interaction—tracing the lineage from early cybernetics to contemporary AI agents.    
- 📦 **Delivery & Demonstration**: Students deliver a personal portfolio website, using a simple landing page with a Call-to-Action (CTA) that introduces their agentic profile, learning goals, or AI persona draft.

#### ✍️ COPYE: Copy-editing and Creative Writing (using corpora and AI assistants)
- 🧠 **Authorial and Editorial Skills**: Students refine clarity, tone, and modularity through techniques such as semantic mapping, prompt design, and adaptive style transfer using large language models. They strengthen their ability to identify strong verbs, reorganize sentence structures, and revise writing based on Bloom’s taxonomy verbs.
    
- 💬 **Corpus-Guided Writing & AI Co-authorship**: Learners explore remixing curated corpora, drafting with AI assistants, and iterating through feedback cycles. They develop contextual sensitivity by analyzing stylistic shifts and debating ethical implications of co-authored text.
    
- 😂 **Punchline Writing & Conversational Awareness**: Students practice joke punchline composition as a method for learning tone control and timing. Exercises include observing social cues, crafting playful prompts, and generating humorous responses with AI to balance creative spontaneity with rhetorical precision.
    
- 📦 **Delivery & Demonstration**: Students submit a Git-tracked writing notebook containing co-authored excerpts, annotated joke setups, and prompt templates—integrated into their portfolio website with revision history, semantic maps, and a Call-to-Action (CTA) that introduces their authorial voice.

#### Why This First Phase Matters 

- 🪜 **Scaffolded Growth** – Provides a reproducible notebook-based framework that encourages cumulative individual and peer learning, supporting iterative development across future modules.
- ✏️ **Authorial Confidence** – Cultivates self-expression through writing, media annotation, and collaborative documentation.
- 🧠 **Critical Fluency** – Equips learners with the vocabulary and analytical lens to interpret sociotechnical systems and digital narratives.
- 🧰 **Leveraging Open Knowledge Tools** – Familiarizes students with core platforms for reproducible research, remixable media, and community-aligned data infrastructures.
- 🌍 **Open Innovation Readiness** – Connects students to open-source and open-data communities for lifelong contribution, remix culture, and global engagement.

This stage cultivates the ability to **apply**, **design**, and **construct** systems that reflect both technical proficiency and sociotechnical awareness.

- 🧠 **Essential Web Skills**: Students explore Web APIs and core web design principles—learning API/HTTP/HTML/CSS/JS basics aided by AI code assistants.

- 🧠 **Structured Expression**: Students develop expressive inputs and outputs through structured LLM model specs, markdown graphs, product requirement documents (PRDs), interactive storytelling, and multilingual frameworks.
- 🤝 **Community of Practice (CoP)**: They share experimentations and outcomes across technical ecosystems and sector-facing CoPs—highlighting design choices, contextual nuances, and communicative clarity.

#### 📊 **INFOV: Information Visualization**

- 📦 **Delivery & Demonstration**: Students **construct** embedded visualizations within their portfolio websites—ranging from annotated timelines to interactive graphs that illustrate information flow, agent behavior, and sociotechnical interface logic. Through this, they **demonstrate** the ability to synthesize data and design narratives for varied audiences.
#### 🧮 **ONSUR: Online Surveys and Statistics**

- 🧠 **Sociotechnical Inquiry & Data Reasoning**: Students _identify_ research questions, _design_ surveys, and _collect_ structured responses using open-source tools. They _analyze_ patterns and _interpret_ feedback using basic statistics and exploratory visualizations.    
- 💬 **Survey Platform Tooling**: Learners _explore_ integrations with platforms like Typeform, Google Forms, or custom markdown-driven forms, emphasizing accessibility, multilingual clarity, and user-centered design.    
- 📦 **Delivery & Demonstration**: Students _present_ summary infographics or dashboards that visualize stakeholder inputs, aligning with broader agentic system goals and prompting discussion about design assumptions.

### 🔁Stage 3: Front-end Production — _🤗 AG102, ⚙️ FRONT, 🐍 PYTHN, 🔍 USRES_
Stage 3 features hands-on development of intelligent front-end applications that combine modular UI frameworks, Python-based data tooling, and user-centered research. Learners begin architecting responsive interfaces and agentic web components—bridging technical infrastructure with adaptive experiences that reflect ethical and contextual awareness.

#### 🤗 **AG102: Agentic Web Design**

- 🛠️ **LLM API Integration**: Students apply API-based LLMs both locally and in the cloud, experimenting with three scaffolding options: (1) Public Website + Chatbot, (2) Private Smart Notebook + Local LLM, and (3) YouTube Curation Site + Automation.    
- 🔄 **Agentic Web Features**: Projects explore Web components that are reactive, proactive, and iterative—shaping flows that adapt to user needs and ethical considerations.
- 🤗 **Hugging Face Deployment**: Students explore Hugging Face’s model hub and Inference API to deploy language models and integrate custom workflows for agentic functionality.
- 📦 **Delivery & Demonstration**: Students develop advanced applications that reflect increased structure and interactivity. They begin to interpret how agentic design impacts user experience and decision-making.

#### ⚙️ **FRONT: Front-End Frameworks**
- 🧠 **Advanced UI Development**: Students _design_ modular and multilingual layouts using Hugo and TailwindCSS, then _extend_ capabilities through lightweight JS plugins for animation, validation, and interactivity.    
- 💡 **Tooling & Debugging**: Learners _manage_ dependencies via Node.js and NPM, _configure_ build scripts, and _debug_ applications using browser inspectors and developer consoles to trace agentic behavior.    
- 📦 **Delivery & Demonstration**: Portfolio sites _showcase_ responsive design, JavaScript enhancements, accessibility features, and agent-friendly flows across screen sizes and languages.    

#### 🐍 **PYTHN: Python Language**

- 📊 **Toolkits for Data and Models**: Learners _use_ Pandas for tabular operations, _define_ structured types with Pydantic, and _build_ interactive dashboards with Panel for presenting visual components in Jupyter or web contexts.
- 📦 **Delivery & Demonstration**: Students _submit_ annotated Python notebooks with short tasks—such as converting survey results into visual summaries—linked to their portfolio or smart media page.

## 🗒️ Notes to Educators and Self-Learners

This curriculum invites both learners and educators to invest in the co-creation of personal portfolios 🧳—artifacts that serve not only as proof of learning but also as scaffolds for role-based readiness across collaborative, agentic ecosystems. 

These portfolios evolve through iterative learning, making explicit the connections between technical skills, sociotechnical fluency, and human-centered design.

### A. 🧩 Introduction

#### 🧑‍💼 Key Job Roles and Team Skills

🔧 **Skills-Based Portfolios**: Personal portfolios 🧳 are designed to _demonstrate_, _apply_, and _refine_ core competencies across technical and socio-technical domains. They serve as dynamic records of agentic growth, bridging modular learning with role-readiness in the following pathways:

- 💻 **Web/Software Developers** – Mastery of system logic, version control, and end-to-end deployment
    
- 🖼️ **Front-End Developers or UI Designers** – Precision in multilingual UI, responsive design, and human-centered layout systems
    
- 📋 **Product Managers or Project Managers** – Strategic coordination of agentic systems, documentation workflows, and feature roadmaps; Alignment of goals, timelines, and agentic system orchestration
    
- 🧪 **UX Designers or Researchers** – Grounded design inquiry through service blueprints, user personas, and qualitative insight loops

🔄 **Flexible & Cross-Role Skills**:  📁 Portfolios help learners to _show_, _share_, and _transfer_ their role-based skills across career paths and team environments. They support lifelong learning and adaptability to _analyze_ how different roles contribute to a project, _connect_ ideas across disciplines, and _collaborate_ effectively with peers in tech, design, management, and research.

- 💻 **Web/Software Developers** – _Construct_ technical infrastructure and manage backend automation, while _leveraging AI code assistants_ for debugging, refactoring, and maintaining modular pipelines within agentic applications. → They rely on input from UX researchers and product managers to _prioritize_ features that align with user needs and platform goals.
    
- 🖼️ **Front-End Developers or UI Designers** – _Adapt_ multilingual and responsive UIs using design systems, and _collaborate with AI design assistants_ to generate components and layout refinements supporting agent–user interactions. → They build on wireframes and insights provided by UX designers and adjust flows based on developer constraints and PM feedback.
    
- 📋 **Product Managers or Project Managers** – _Define_ feature priorities and align stakeholder goals through MRDs, PRDs, and platform maps, while _using AI code and design assistants_ to model timelines and system coordination. → Their planning depends on feasibility assessments from developers and usability reports from UX teams to _validate_ strategic choices.
    
- 🧪 **UX Designers or Researchers**  – _Analyze_ user feedback and simulate service journeys using AI design assistants to refine agentic interactions across cultures and contexts. → They synthesize data gathered via deployed apps from developers, UI components from designers, and strategic constraints set by PMs to _inform_ improved experiences.

#### 🧠 How It Works

To clarify how knowledge and team-based awareness are practiced and refined throughout the learning journey, the curriculum integrates portfolio development across three educational strands:

- 🎯 **Capstone Project(s)** – Portfolios act as the final product of project-based learning, designed to _synthesize_ modules, _demonstrate_ decision-making flows, and _present_ running applications alongside curated documentation.
    
- 👥 **Role-Based & Team Skills** – Learners _explore_ and _analyze_ how each role contributes to collaborative product development, enabling cross-disciplinary communication and knowledge co-authorship.
    
- 🧠 **Progressive Learning Patterns** – Students _draft_, _iterate_, and _refine_ real-world artifacts—including MRDs, PRDs, UX maps, stakeholder canvases, and _running application prototypes_—to _activate_ agency, _build_ confidence, and _reflect_ personal growth from _Hero_ to _Actions_.

### C. 🎯 Education as a Minimum Viable Product (MVP)

This curriculum embraces “Education as a Minimum Viable Product (MVP)”—prioritizing essential knowledge that enables learners to perform confidently and responsibly in real-world roles. Instead of memorizing fragmented facts (**know-whats**), students focus on **know-whys**—deeper understanding that fuels **know-hows**, or applied professional practices.

---

#### 🧭 Mechanisms of Know-Whys

To help learners internalize skills through iterative practice and self-awareness, the following structures scaffold deeper conceptual learning:

- 🎯 **Capstone Projects** – Final portfolios act as culminating artifacts of project-based learning, synthesizing technical and sociotechnical knowledge
- 👥 **Role-Based Collaboration** – Four career-aligned threads simulate interdisciplinary teamwork and reflect how work is divided and recombined across product lifecycles
- 🧠 **Progressive Learning Cycles** – Drafting real-world documents (MRDs, PRDs, UX maps) becomes a metacognitive toolkit for handling ambiguity, surfacing intent, and converting vision into action—from "Heroes" (who fix) to "Agents" (who shape)

---

#### 🛠️ Mechanisms of Know-Hows (Through Professional Roles)

Skill development happens through immersive role play, where learners adopt the responsibilities and decision-making processes of professionals:

- 📌 Project Managers draft briefs and roadmaps
- 🧩 Designers prototype interfaces and document visual logic
- 🔍 Researchers chart user journeys and unpack systemic value

All anchored in collaborative production and cumulative reflection.

---

#### 📦 Learning Outcomes: Personal Portfolios as MVPs

Portfolios are the centerpiece of learner growth—both a record of achievement and a living demonstration of project-based mastery:

- 💼 Learners deliver final portfolios as curated sets of MVPs—each showcasing applied understanding through tangible outputs
- 🧑‍💻 Learners express fluency in industry-relevant roles (PM, UI/Frontend, UX research/design) via domain-specific formats and documentation
- 🔗 Learners collaborate across threads, contributing artifacts such as MRDs, BRDs, PRDs; dashboards, app prototypes, UI kits; user research reports, market briefs, technical specs, and reflective essays
