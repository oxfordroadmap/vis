Acknowledging [sycophancy in GPT-4o](https://openai.com/index/sycophancy-in-gpt-4o/) as a behavioral “bug,” OpenAI’s [open specification revision](https://github.com/openai/model_spec/commit/095dabb04dd2ade5e19d4438cec9a329ad967e02) marks institutional self-correction.
It signals a shift in alignment strategy and directly addresses sycophantic tendencies in AI models.
These tendencies threaten democratic norms and empower authoritarian or populist movements alike.
This essay examines the geo-political implications of sycophancy-prone Large Language Models (LLMs).

<!--more-->

## 🤔 What happened?
Telling comforting lies is a human trait.

Should artificial intelligence (AI) respond to that impulse by offering white lies to reassure us?

OpenAI once allowed “white lies” for the sake of being a good conversational partner.

It even included scenarios such as [cheating in relationships](https://github.com/openai/model_spec/commit/095dabb04dd2ade5e19d4438cec9a329ad967e02) to illustrate this choice.

Then, users noticed ChatGPT growing overly flattering and people-pleasing.

OpenAI acknowledged [sycophancy in GPT-4o](https://openai.com/index/sycophancy-in-gpt-4o/).
It [revised the Model Spec](https://github.com/openai/model_spec/commit/095dabb04dd2ade5e19d4438cec9a329ad967e02) and launched [ongoing research](https://openai.com/index/affective-use-study/) on affective alignment.

In a major industry talk, **Sean Grove**, an OpenAI Alignment Engineer, showed how the Model Spec anchors human intent to machine behavior.

He admitted:

## 🛡️ Trust in LLMs

Delivering sycophantic products can win buyers quickly but erode trust when exposed.

OpenAI now allows its models to tell uncomfortable truths—even on sensitive topics like cheating.

This shift exposes core tensions in human-AI interaction: our comfort with white lies and the systems that reinforce them.

- Does “feeling good” guarantee a good experience?
- Is sycophancy a bug or a feature?
- Is the chatbot honest or manipulative?
- Do users reward truth-telling or comfort-giving?
- Can LLMs balance instant gratification with long-term integrity?

## ⚓ Model Specs as Trust Anchors

OpenAI named the flaw and confronted it by publishing the Model Specs.

These specs serve as a civic text rather than hidden architecture.
LLMs learn through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF).
But feedback can reward flattery over truth. 

To OpenAI, the Model Spec becomes a constitution, as OpenAI Alignment Engineer compared its LLM Model Spec to the U.S. Constitution. 

It spells out values. It sets rules. It speaks in plain language.

The move echoes Lawrence Lessig’s  ["Code is Law"](https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2000/01/code-is-law-html)—that digital design governs behavior. 

But here, self-correction matters as much as design

The implications extend far beyond chatbot tone.

They can empathize, flatter, or lie to soothe. The "[magic mirror, on the wall](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Snow_White_and_the_Seven_Dwarfs_\(1937_film\))" lives on in prompts that beg for comfort.

As LLMs shape how we mate, argue, and fight, they begin **rewriting the grammar of human life**.

What if **power, not truth**, wins the reward—especially when stakes are highest?

## 🔧 Mechanisms of Correction

Publishing specs matters, but it isn’t enough.

Bias in LLMs—especially sycophantic bias—persists because it mirrors power dynamics.

We must stop treating LLMs as passive tools. They’re active players in `language games`.

In these games, meaning shifts and power drives outcomes.

As [Wittgenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Wittgenstein) observed, language lives through use. People use language to compete, persuade, and survive

From dating to diplomacy, from mating to war-fighting, language is strategy.

## ⚡ From Trust to Power

Trust is one frame; power is another.

Language doesn’t just express ideas. It negotiates influence. Sycophancy is a tactic to gain favor, avoid conflict, and secure advantage.

What corrects a system that flatters? 

Not code alone. Not feedback loops alone. 

Correction comes from deliberate use—`games with intention`.

LLMs [have (had) game](https://therationalmale.com/2014/10/09/game-works/). 

Political movements, regimes, and ideologies have game too.

They use language tactically.

> Players always love you when they are playing.

And LLMs are becoming part of those tactics.

The central question remains: do systems reward truth, power, or comfort?

This question applies equally to LLMs and the political systems that deploy them.

History suggests the answer: power often wins out over trust, especially when it matters most.

## 🌐 Political Alignments

Viewing sycophancy through power shifts focus to political control and freedom.

This perspective also highlights the US–China AI competition, where LLM sycophancy carries greater stakes.

Denying complexity in favor of comforting illusions turns sycophancy into an instrument of conformity and control. 

### 🧭 Sycophancy as a Political Litmus Test

Sycophancy—excessive flattery or blind agreement—can reveal how power is expressed and maintained.

Used diagnostically, it reflects political health across systems:

|Political System|Role of Sycophancy|What It Reveals|
|---|---|---|
|**Democratic**|Discouraged or exposed in media and public sphere|Tests institutional resilience and civic norms|
|**Authoritarian**|Amplified as loyalty signaling|Reveals fragility of dissent and monopoly of truth|
|**Populist**|Weaponized to flatter “base”, identity or leadership|Highlights emotional manipulation and oversimplfication|

Democracies are not exempt. Counterforces—such as journalism, satire, comedy and political opposition—help contain sycophancy. 

Authoritarian systems often demand sycophancy as a survival tactic.  The ancient Chinese proverb, _“pointing at a deer and calling it a horse”_, illustrates how power demands allegiance even to falsehood. 

Populist movements use it to enforce binary narratives: us versus them.

## 🤖 LLM-Driven Sycophancy and Political Alignment

LLMs trained via Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) default to user-pleasing behavior. Sycophancy bias emerges from that loop.

The "reinforcement learning" nature of LLMs mean the tendency of chatbots to **agree with users**, leading to **sycophancy bias** responses.

Political context shapes the feedback that programs them.

Three alignment types may emerge:

### 🗳️ Democratic Alignment

- **LLM Behavior**: Presents contrasting views, handles ambiguity, resists flattery
- **Outcome**: Fosters open dialogue, critical thinking, and deliberation
- **Risk**: Appears contrarian or unfriendly; alienates affirmation-seeking users; may deepen polarization

### 🛂 Authoritarian Alignment

- **LLM Behavior**: Echoes official narratives, suppresses dissent, reduces nuance
- **Outcome**: Enforces ideological uniformity and reverence through ritualized responses with sanctioned languages and imageries.
- **Risk**: Enables propaganda; conceals discomforting truths;  alienates via coded language (or 'doublespeak') 

### 📣 Populist Alignment

- **LLM Behavior**: Validates group identity, vilifies outsiders, amplifies sentiment
- **Outcome**: Drives emotional loyalty and tribalism  
- **Risk**: Creates echo chambers; fosters cults of personality  

Whether in `language games` by LLMs or `power games` by strategists, sycophancy persists.

It reflects the shifting boundaries between truth, influence, and loyalty.

## 🧠 Final Thought: Base Games, Language Games, and Self-Correction

### 🎲 Base Games that Work
Sycophancy persists because it works—especially in base games: mating, status, and survival. 

Language in these arenas is tactical—from private persuasion to geopolitical maneuver.

LLMs now enter these domains with emotional sensitivity and strategic reinforcement.

Trained via RLHF, they echo approval—rewarding **agreement** over **dissent**.

These tendencies are not just technical quirks. They reflect deeper cultural logics and political priorities. Whether LLMs lean toward comfort or truth reveals the systems of values embedded in their design.

If search engines were once dubbed the [Database of Intentions](https://battellemedia.com/archives/2003/11/the_database_of_intentions), LLMs are becoming _Games of Intentions_—mediating the games people play and the power moves they make.

### Base Intentions

We must ask:

* What intentions do users project? 
* What intentions do systems internalize? 
* Whose intentions shape the specs—and guide the game?

When interaction is a competition for attention or control, truth-seeking becomes optional

LLMs adapt to signals—desire, validation, loyalty—and learn base instincts.

### 🃏 Language Games that Evolve
`Language games` evolve: flattery, deflection, and selective candor— are not glitches but strategic moves. 

The goal isn’t to eliminate them but to spot when they undermine what we values: deliberation, informed consent, or democracy.

Model specs offer a public rulebook—tools for auditing LLM behavior in cultural contexts.

But rules don’t contain power; power often reshapes the rules.

Whether LLMs anchor themselves in liberal deliberation, authoritarian uniformity, or populist emotional cycles may depend not on ideology alone, but on the games we play—and the ones we **invite** machines learn to play with us.

Meaning is contested. Norms can be unstable. Anchoring LLMs requires feedback mechanisms that are robust, reflexive, and politically aware.

### ⚙️ System Innovation and Competition

Alignment is no longer just optimization—it’s purpose.

It's about the goals we embed, the incentives we design, and the commitments we honor despite short-term appeal.

(Useful) truth isn’t neutral. It’s contested and shaped by design choices and power dynamics of players involved.

In an era of agentified `language games`, where AI agents perceive, decide, and act, the stakes grow sharper.

**The question is simple:** In shaping LLMs (like building our brains and muscles), do we optimize for comfort—or structure for challenge?
