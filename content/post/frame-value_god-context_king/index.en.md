How can we _breathe new life into_ AI applications or projects? This article introduces a practical framework that spotlights the **game-like** nature of LLM-driven AIs, drawing on Ludwig Wittgenstein’s concepts of `language-games` and `form of life`.

For the next generation of AI engineers and creators, this approach may invite a playfully serious reflection on **values**.

I have ***game***!

<!--more-->

**When does word-generation become value-generation?**

### 🎲 Three Sentences That Frame It All

1. I have ***game***.  
2. You read my mind?  
3. Game on.

Each distills the essence of human value, artificial intelligence, and shared meaning-making.

## 1. Essence of Game

> I have ***game***.

Three words. They express playfulness, attraction, confidence, and the serious pursuit of value and relationship.

Historically, men have anchored success in `muscles, money, game, (and frame)` (Source: [Build muscle, make money, learn Game](https://x.com/RationalMale/status/1355328296779710466)). The life playbook may sound trite, but it still outlines core dynamics.

These patterns point toward the [fundamentals of relationships and relational value](https://www.amazon.com/Value-Others-Understanding-Relationships-Marketplace-ebook/dp/B0D1Q5LHNV). 

People collaborate. People compete. People want to be **useful to others**.

Game, in this sense, is timeless—whether embodied in hunter-gatherer muscles, urban-life money, dating, negotiation, or geopolitical maneuvering. It expresses a core human pursuit: **value through interaction.**

## 2. Essence of Large Language Models

> You read my mind?

LLMs are token-prediction machines. Their magic lies in anticipating the next word based on massive corpora.

That means they mainly simulate—rather than compute—understanding. 

They “play” language at scale. 

They can offer [flatteries of emotional values]({{% ref "/post/insights-shipping_sycophancy_erodes_trust/" %}})). 

To some, they can even offer [snake-oil like promises]({{% ref "post/insights-ai_snake_oil_consequences/" %}}) when trust has begun crumbling in different corners of the society. 

They compose prose, translate texts, generate answers. But unlike logicians, they don’t follow mathematical rigor (à la Bertrand Russell). They model language _as used_, not as idealized logic.

And now, they often outperform humans in human expressions. Not because they understand, but because they predict (your mind) well.

“You read my mind?” is our reflex response when a machine nails it. But beneath this ‘aha!’ moment is often just well-trained mimicry of statistical patterns.

So: **When does word-generation become value-generation?**

## 3. Machines Shaping the Essence of Language Games

### ♞ Next word and next move: use value

Machines almost ***read*** our minds by ***guessing*** our next words.

Game players guess the next moves.

Still, beyond words, man as the burden to prove themselves.

> “A man can never become just a mouth!”

This slogan from Taiwanese TV mocks hollow masculinity—reminding us that words alone are not enough.

Men have to perform.

### ♛ LLMs' value in value-exchange 

If the goal is value creation, then LLMs likely participate in all kinds of games that human beings have been playing.

Wittgenstein saw meaning not as internal representation of logics, but as **language use within shared practices**—what he called `language-games`.

In *Philosophical Investigations*, he illustrates that a single word like “Water!” can mean a warning, a request, or a reply—depending on the surrounding context and shared understanding.

**Meaning is use. In community. In context.**

---

### ♚ Context is King!

To make LLMs truly useful, we need `context`—a scaffolding of digital resources, including system instructions, memory, and tools.

If the goal is value creation through interaction, then context becomes critical.

Several innovations to frame LLMs now emerge:

- `Context windows`: Crucial for token prediction in LLMs  
- `Model Context Protocol` [introduced in Nov 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_Context_Protocol): Defines useful tools and assets around LLMs  
- `Context Engineering`: A growing field that goes beyond prompt design to build usable AI agents (See [one technical doc](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/context-engineering-what-it-is-and-techniques-to-consider))

Together, these efforts align with Wittgenstein’s insight on **rule-following** in `language games`.  

He observed that following a rule requires a shared standard among a community of speakers, or game-players.

---

### ♝ Value Is God?

LLMs alone cannot “learn game” from language data. Without context, no value emerges.

To participate in human activities, LLMs must engage **multiple prompts**, diverse `language-games`.

Wittgenstein would caution us against assigning “understanding” to such systems—just as one might hesitate when a person claims to understand someone fully in a fleeting moment of attraction or persuasion.

It is like a game when a woman play to a man: "I understand you."  Or when a man play to a woman: "I feel you."

Which leads to the deeper question:  
**What is the source of value?**

---

### ♟Grounding (Language) Games

So what do AI engineers and product managers really do?

They groom AI to participate in human games, games of values.

We must resist elevating mere outputs—whether from a chatbot, a psychic, a politician, or a salesperson—as evidence of true care, intelligence, or intent.

Premature awe can be manipulation.

### 🧬 _Lebensform_ `form of life` 
To ground LLMs meaningfully, we return to Wittgenstein's concept of `form of life` (German: _Lebensform_).

Meaning isn’t abstract—it arises from **shared human activities**, rooted in social norms, practical habits, and relational context.

It sets the stage for a deeper question in AI engineering:

> How can we _breathe new life into_ AI applications?

Languages occur in-between breaths.

This calls the next generation of AI engineers not just to process text and other modalities of human expressions, but to build systems that **join human games**—from dating to diplomacy.

Language has scaled civilizations because it reshapes relationships at scale and at increasingly complexity—and the rules we play by.

Game on.
