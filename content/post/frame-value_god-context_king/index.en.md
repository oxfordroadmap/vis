How can one _breathe (new) life into_ AI applications or projects? This article shares a useful and practical framework that highlights the "game" aspect of the LLM-driven AIs, using the concepts of `language-games` and `form of life` by Ludwig Wittgenstein.  In addition, 
for the next generation of AI engineers and innovators, it can inspire a few playful yet serious thinking of **values**.  I have ***game***!

<!--more-->

In short, the overall thinking framework can be condensed into three following sentences, each of which explains the essence of `game`, `Large Language Models`, and `Language Games`.

1. I have ***game***!
2. You read my mind?
3. Game on.

## Essence of Game

> I have ***game***!

These three words sum up the values of humans (and relationships), with all its playfulness and seriousness.

`Muscles, money, game, (and frame)` have been the core belief and observations of men for a thriving life. [Build muscle, make money, learn game](https://x.com/RationalMale/status/1355328296779710466). Life's playbook appear trite. 

They nevertheless point to the [fundamental of relationships, the values of others](https://www.amazon.com/Value-Others-Understanding-Relationships-Marketplace-ebook/dp/B0D1Q5LHNV). People compete, and people collaborate.  Be useful to others.  

The essence of game thus, in the forms of muscles in hunter-gather communities, in the forms of money in market relationships, and in the various dating, war-fighting, and power-grabbing games, marks the endless ingenuity of the same game: human values and relationships.

## Essence of Large Language Models

> You read my mind?

Large-Language Models (LLMs), as a form of artificial intelligence, feature the capability of guessing (or predicting) the next word in a sequence of human language expressions. 

LLMs, as next token-prediction games, effectively “playing” a minimalist language-game. 

They do generate prose, compose essays, translate languages, or answer questions.  

They however, do not follow logician and mathematician (e.g., Bertrand Russell) rules, but mimic human language expressions.  

LLMs can now beat human beings in human expressions, on the grounds of the vast existing corpora and the fast speed.  

Machines almost read our minds by guessing our next words.  

"You read my mind?" A human being might hit the [Aha! moment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka_effect) when LLMs solve problems.  

One can also be more practical (and less philosophical and/or political) to observe, at that moment of transaction, LLMs provide something useful, and something of values. 

These generated words might be just mere statistical patterns in massive text corpora. They might just recycle answers from the existing human knowledge.  

What is the line between word-generation versus value-generation?

## Essence of Language Games

“A man can never become just a mouth!” 

This was a commercial slogan in Taiwan that mocks men for being useless (to women), closer to the notion that action speaks louder than words. 

Here, we begin to hit the practical question regarding AI engineering based on LLMs:

> How can one _breathe (new) life into_ AI applications or projects? 

When we go through the route of "game", i.e., the individual exploration journeys to seek the values of others, and thus interpersonal, familial, professional, industrial, social, and even international relationships, LLMs become useful and playful tools. 

[Ludwig Wittgenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Wittgenstein), a philosopher just a generation before [Alan Turing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Turing "Alan Turing"), provides useful insights with the notions of `language-games` and `form of life`.

### Context is King!

Departing form the traditional views of language as mental representations, Wittgenstein argues that meaning comes from how words function within `language-games` .

In Philosophical Investigations, he demonstrated that an utterance like “Water!” can serve as a warning, an answer or an order, depending on the surrounding game and its participant-players shared practices.  

Meaning is use. In shared practices (that serve people). In context.

Games are contextual so as to derive meaning (and thus values for human participants).  

In this perspective, context is king in `language-games`.  

Now with the advancement of LLMs, its usefulness depends on the `context` defined surrounding LLMs. 

In other words, for LLMs to be useful, a `context` describing the digital resources and tools is required to make LLMs participate in the games people find valuable.  

Several LLM terms highlight Wittgenstein's `language-games` foresight: 

* `Context Windows` are critical in the next token prediction by contextualizing word meaning
* The `Model Context Protocol`, [introduced in November 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_Context_Protocol) to specify useful and usable resources and tools as context
* `Context Engineering`, increasingly [a branch of AI engineering to build effective AI agents](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/context-engineering-what-it-is-and-techniques-to-consider), advances and departs from “prompt engineering”

These LLM efforts highlight the importance of context in deriving meaning (and values).  

Context for LLM now include: the system prompt/instruction, the user input, short and long term memory, relevant knowledge base, and tools.  

Here, these LLM context efforts fit well with what Wittgenstein thinks as **rule-following**  in `language-games`.  He observed that following a rule requires a shared standard among a community of speakers.   

In essence, what AI engineers do is to "groom" AI to participate in the games human find valuable.

### Value is God?

An LLM cannot just `learn game` with large language data by itself. No context, and no value.

For an LLM to partake in real human activities, an LLM must engage multiple prompts, or tiny `language-games`.   

From my understanding, Wittgenstein would urge caution against attributing true “understanding” to LLMs.  

It is similar to urge caution against attributing love and affections when a woman claims that she understands a man. 

This lead to the fundamental question regarding values, and the source of values. 

It is advisable, thus from the notion of `language-games`, not to hastily take any utterance of an educated guess of an LLM, a psychic, a politician, a sale, etc., as understanding, caring, knowledgeable, or even omniscient. 

Hasty elevation can be manipulation.  

### The ground games

Grounding LLMs as language-games that support human activities is critical once we read Wittgenstein‘s notion of [form of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Form_of_life) (German: _Lebensform_). 

Wittgenstein‘s focus on language-games as the operating machine of meaning emphasizes 
the shared human activities and practices, and ways of living. These lived practices—activities suffused with shared values, norms, and backgrounds that shape both competition and collaboration. 

For the next generation of AI engineers and innovators, this translates into designing systems that don’t just process text, but genuinely participate in the human “language‐games” that shape our world.

AI are expected to impact dating, professional, political and international relationships through the same-old games.  Languages have scaled and partitioned human groups and innovations by reshaping relationships and value systems. 

Game on.
